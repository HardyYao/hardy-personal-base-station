今年加入了 flomo 的领航员社群，每周四下午，是 flomo 领航员的答题时间，这周的问题是：遇到左右为难的决定时，你会不会按某些固定的步骤或问题来帮自己想清楚?比如会问自己哪些问题?

这几个月，工作有点忙，工作之余自己也有很多感兴趣的事情要做，所以好久没回答领航员的问题了。

不过今天这个问题，我比较喜欢，刚好下午也提前完成了工作任务，就抽空写了一下自己对于这个问题的答案。

遇到左右为难的决定时，我一般会做三件事：

1、持续记录与之相关的事情。

2、在记录的过程中一边深入思考，一边回顾自己记录过的内容，从中找到一些线索，然后对自己提出一些关键问题。

3、回答完自己提出的关键问题后，基本上就知道该做出什么决定了。

举个例子：

在上家公司的最后一年里，我因为先前已经连续参与了好多个没有意义的烂项目，而且也不喜欢公司的某些领导刻意营造出来的那种“大家都要自觉加班”的压抑氛围，导致我在工作中完全找不到成就感、也过得非常难受，特别想辞职。

但是那两年的互联网就业行情又不太好，而当时我在上一家公司，已经是属于骨干成员，工作内容都比较熟悉了，部门总监也正准备帮我申请：升职加薪。

所以，是继续忍受我所厌恶的那种工作环境、熬下去？还是辞职出去外面看看其他机会？对于当时的我来说，是一个比较困难的抉择问题。

也恰好是在2023年，我开始频繁使用 flomo 并且购买了会员。

那一年，我在 flomo 上面也记录了不少工作相关的事情，某天，我在使用 flomo 的每日回顾功能回顾之前的笔记时，发现自己但凡记录到跟前公司有关的事情，都不是什么积极正向的内容。

我从中意识到，继续留在那个地方，只会不断地消耗掉我的心力，让我失去原有的朝气与活力，所以我下定决心要离开那里。

决定要从上家公司辞职后，我就对自己提出一个很关键的问题：下一份工作，我想要找什么类型的？

某天，我无意间在 flomo 翻到半年多以前的一条笔记：我的下一份工作，要找跟制造业相关的开发工作。

然后我回顾了自己以往的工作经历，发现除了前公司的那份工作，我的其它工作经历，都是跟制造业相关的。

我似乎也挺适合去开发制造业相关的系统，这类系统，往往对于技术并没有太高要求，但是很重视业务流程。而梳理清楚业务逻辑、输出业务流程图和业务说明文档，刚好都是我比较擅长的事情，也是我比较喜欢做的事情。

熟悉了制造业的业务知识，将来就算不做开发了，转型去做业务专家、去做方案专家或者是其他管理岗位，也都是有机会的。

所以，我希望自己的下一份工作，要尽量往制造业方向靠拢。

最终，关于这个问题，我得出的结论大致如下：

2.1、最优选项：找一份开发制造业信息管理系统相关的新工作，并且所在团队要很有发展前景，也能够给出比较有竞争力的薪资待遇。

2.2、普通选项：如果实在找不到制造业的岗位，也可以继续在互联网行业找，但不要是外包公司，可以是有发展潜力的初创公司，也可以是较为成熟的中大型公司，关键是要有稳定的项目或产品可以做。

2.3、最后选项：如果以上两种都找不到，我允许自己有几个月的职场空窗期，一边找工作，一边修养身心，但是一旦超过一定时间范围，就不要挑了，外包什么的也行，先有份工作再说。

到这里，我对于先前的抉择问题，已经有了很明确的答案了。

去年（2024年）过完年之后没多久，在还没找到新工作之前，我就跟领导提了离职，开始将自己的想法付诸行动了。

很幸运，最终，我的新工作基本是符合最优选项的。

虽然不是什么知名大厂，但是工作内容是我比较感兴趣的，工作压力也不大，也不用被迫去卷各种没有意义的事情。

有事情可以做，也有自己的时间可以去体验生活，我很喜欢目前的工作和生活状态。

以上是我今天下午回答 flomo 领航员问题的答案，而此刻，我又有了一些与之相关的想法。那就是：持续产生负面能量的工作环境，对人的侵蚀力是超乎想象的。

上家公司的糟糕经历给我造成的负面影响，一直到了最近才算是消除得七七八八，而我已经从那边离职有一年半的时间了。

之前，我在上班期间，只要工作任务还没完成，大多数时候，我的专注度是非常高的，很少会看新闻、刷知乎、聊微信、听音乐，等等。

而在上家公司最后一年多的时间里，上班刷新闻、逛知乎、聊微信、听音乐几乎成为了我的上班日常，而这并没有给我带来多少快乐，反而加剧了我的内耗。

如果我参与的项目是能够落地的、是能够给自己带来成就感的、是能够给公司带来收益的，那么我会很用心地去做，可是在上家公司最后一年多的时间里，我做过的项目大部分都不是这种类型。不光如此，有些领导还逼着大家去做这些没意义的事情，还天天鼓励自觉加班、逼着大家去卷，团队的氛围极其压抑，所以在很长的一段时间里，我都无法再像之前那样认真去做事。

那些负面影响，也带到了我的新工作中来。

我现在的工作环境，相对来说是很宽松的，刚入职一年多的时间里，我几乎没加过班，哪怕这几个月偶尔需要加加班，但跟上一份工作的强度相比起来，那简直就是“小巫见大巫”。

我们部门的总监也很有担当，帮我扛了很多压力，即便开发进度慢了，也是他去挨总经理的骂，很少把压力直接传递到我这边来。

虽说周六上午要上半天班这一点我很不喜欢，可这边是上午九点上班、下午五点半下班，然后下完班之后，只要不是生产环境的系统出现问题，是从来不会有人打电话找我的。不加班的话，实际一周上班时间还不到四十小时。

而且在这边，我是知道自己开发的系统都会有实际用户、都会产生价值的。

相比起来，上家公司，虽然名义上是双休，可是我周末的时候过得一点都不开心，因为我永远不知道周末的时候会不会突然就有个电话来叫我去处理一些毫无意义的“紧急问题”，而且每次过完周末，一想到自己又要去做一大堆毫无意义的工作，我就会觉得很疲惫。

可是去年入职以来，我对于自己上班期间的注意力集中度，一直都非常不满意，因为上一份工作的一些坏习惯，我一时之间很难改掉，这个问题也困扰了我挺久的。

一直到最近一两个月，我才能够在大多数时候保持上班期间很专注做事的感觉，偶尔如果觉得状态不太好，我也会选择休息或者写点文字，而非刷各种无意义的信息流。

我完全不敢想象，如果继续留在上家公司，那么再过个几年，我是否还能拥有那种专注做事的状态？我是否还有走出来的勇气？

我很庆幸自己去年做出了从上家公司辞职的决定，虽然这让我每年损失了不小的一笔钱（我上一份工作，算上当时领导为了挽留我，要给我提的额外加薪待遇，一年的薪资会比现在这份工作高出不少），但我觉得这很值得。

至少我现在，过得很开心！

## What to Do When You Face a Difficult Decision

This year, I joined the Flomo Navigator Community.

Every Thursday afternoon, members answer a weekly question.

This week’s question was:

“When you face a difficult decision, do you follow certain steps or ask yourself specific questions to think more clearly? What kind of questions do you ask yourself?”

Recently, I’ve been quite busy with work, and I also have many personal interests to focus on, so it has been a while since I last answered a Navigator question.

But I liked today’s topic, and since I finished my work early, I took some time to write my answer.

---

### What I Usually Do When I’m Torn Between Two Choices

When I face a difficult decision, I usually do three things:

1. Keep recording anything related to the decision.
2. While recording, think deeply and review what I’ve written to find clues, then ask myself key questions.
3. After answering those key questions, I usually know what choice to make.

---

### An Example from My Past

In my last year at my previous company, I had already worked on several meaningless projects.

I also disliked the work atmosphere — some leaders tried to create a stressful “everyone must work overtime” culture.

Because of that, I felt no sense of achievement and was very unhappy. I wanted to quit badly.

However, at that time, the job market in the internet industry was poor.

And in that company, I was already a core team member, familiar with most of the systems, and my director was preparing to recommend me for a promotion and a raise.

So I was torn:

Should I stay in a place I disliked but where I was stable?

Or should I leave and take the risk of finding something new?

It was a tough decision.

Around that time — in 2023 — I started using Flomo regularly and even bought a membership.

That year, I recorded many work-related thoughts in Flomo.

One day, when I used the Daily Review feature, I realized that every note related to my old company was negative.

From that, I understood one thing:

If I stayed there, I would only lose more energy, passion, and motivation.

So, I decided to leave.

---

### Asking Myself Key Questions

After deciding to resign, I asked myself a key question:

“What kind of job do I want next?”

Later, I found a note I had written over half a year earlier in Flomo. It said:

“My next job should be related to system development in the manufacturing industry.”

I looked back at my career and realized that, except for my last job, all my previous work experience had been connected to manufacturing.

I also noticed that I was quite suited to developing systems for manufacturing companies.

Such systems don’t require extremely advanced technical skills but rely heavily on understanding business processes — something I’m good at and enjoy doing.

Once I become familiar with manufacturing workflows, I could later move from software development to roles like business expert, solution architect, or even management.

So I decided that my next job should move closer toward the manufacturing industry.

---

### My Final Plan

After thinking carefully, I came up with three options:

2.1 Best Option:

Find a new job developing manufacturing information management systems — with a promising team and competitive pay.

2.2 Normal Option:

If I can’t find a manufacturing job, stay in the internet industry — but not in outsourcing.

It could be a startup with growth potential or a medium-to-large company with stable projects or products.

2.3 Last Option:

If neither works, allow myself a few months off to rest and look for jobs.

But if the break becomes too long, stop being picky — even outsourcing is fine.

The key is to stay active and keep moving forward.

At that point, my decision became clear.

---

### Taking Action

Soon after the Spring Festival in 2024, before finding a new job, I officially told my manager that I would resign.

I started to take real action.

Fortunately, the new job I later found matched my best option quite well.

It’s not a big-name company, but the work is interesting, the pressure is low, and I don’t need to take part in meaningless “competition.”

I have things to do, and I also have time to enjoy life.

I truly like my current work and life balance.

---

### My Reflection After Writing That Answer

After finishing my answer for Flomo today, I had some extra thoughts:

A work environment that constantly produces negative energy can slowly destroy a person.

The bad experiences from my previous job took me over a year to recover from —

even though I’ve been away from that company for more than eighteen months.

Before that, I used to focus very well at work.

I rarely checked news, browsed Zhihu, or listened to music during work hours.

But in my last year there, reading news, chatting online, and listening to music became my daily routine — not because I was relaxed, but because I was drained.

Those habits didn’t make me happy; they only increased my mental exhaustion.

Most of the projects I worked on back then were meaningless and never completed.

Some leaders even forced us to do such useless work and encouraged “voluntary overtime.”

The whole atmosphere was suffocating.

I lost my motivation and stopped taking work seriously.

Those negative habits followed me into my new job.

---

### Recovering from the Past

Now, my current workplace is much better.

In the past year, I’ve rarely worked overtime.

Even when I do occasionally, it’s nothing compared to before.

My department director also takes responsibility — he shields me from upper management’s pressure.

Even when progress is slow, he’s the one who deals with it instead of pushing it down to me.

Although I dislike working half-days on Saturdays, our normal schedule is from 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m.

After work, no one calls unless there’s a real emergency.

Without overtime, my actual workweek is less than forty hours.

Here, I know the systems I develop have real users and real value.

At my old job, even though it was officially a five-day week, I never felt relaxed on weekends.

I was always worried I’d get a call to handle some meaningless “urgent issue.”

Every Sunday night, I felt anxious thinking about another week of useless work.

After joining my new company, however, I noticed that my focus during work hours wasn’t as good as before.

The bad habits from my previous job were hard to shake off.

It bothered me for a long time.

Only in the past couple of months have I started regaining my concentration.

When I feel tired now, I rest or write instead of scrolling through endless information.

---

### The Value of a Difficult Decision

I can’t imagine what would have happened if I had stayed in my previous company —

Would I still be able to focus?

Would I still have the courage to leave someday?

I’m truly glad I made the decision to quit.

Although it cost me a significant amount of money —

since my former boss had planned to offer me a raise to stay —

it was absolutely worth it.

At least now, I’m genuinely happy.
